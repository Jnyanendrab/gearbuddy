<!-- How to Check Price History -->
<section aria-labelledby="price-history">
  <h2 id="price-history">How to check price history before you buy</h2>
  <p>Not every "sale" is a real discount. Some sellers raise the reference price weeks before a promotion so the markdown looks bigger than it is. Two free tools make it easy to verify whether a deal is genuine.</p>
  <ol>
    <li>
      <strong>Install the Keepa browser extension.</strong> Keepa is free and available for Chrome, Firefox, and Edge. Download it from <a href="https://keepa.com" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">keepa.com</a>. Once installed, it embeds a price history chart directly into every Amazon product page — no extra tab or site visit required.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Read the 90-day and 1-year price chart.</strong> On the Keepa chart, look at how the current "sale" price compares to the average price over the past 90 days. A genuine deal will be noticeably below that average. If the price has been flat for months, the "deal" is cosmetic.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Cross-check with CamelCamelCamel.</strong> Go to <a href="https://camelcamelcamel.com" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">camelcamelcamel.com</a>, paste the Amazon product URL, and view the full price history. CamelCamelCamel also lets you set price drop alerts so you get an email when an item hits your target price.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Watch for "List Price" inflation.</strong> Some sellers raise the reference ("List Price") weeks before a sale to make the percentage discount appear larger. The Keepa chart will show this clearly — you'll see a sudden spike in the list price just before the sale event.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Compare against the all-time low.</strong> Both Keepa and CamelCamelCamel display the product's all-time lowest price. A sale price near the all-time low is genuinely good. A sale price that's still above the 90-day average is not worth rushing to buy.
    </li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section aria-labelledby="buy-or-wait">
  <h2 id="buy-or-wait">Buy now or wait for Black Friday?</h2>
  <p>Not every category benefits equally from Black Friday. Here's a quick breakdown by product type.</p>

  <dl>
    <dt>Ergonomic mice</dt>
    <dd><strong>Wait for Black Friday.</strong> Mice are one of the most reliably discounted categories during the holiday sale window.</dd>

    <dt>Monitor light bars</dt>
    <dd><strong>It depends.</strong> Premium models (like the BenQ ScreenBar Halo) rarely drop much. Budget models (like the Baseus) drop more frequently. Wait if you want a budget bar; buy when ready if you want premium.</dd>

    <dt>GaN chargers and power banks</dt>
    <dd><strong>Wait.</strong> This is one of the most competitive categories, with steep seasonal discounts as brands fight for market share.</dd>

    <dt>Mechanical keyboards</dt>
    <dd><strong>Wait.</strong> Keyboards consistently see some of the deepest percentage discounts during Black Friday.</dd>

    <dt>Cable management accessories</dt>
    <dd><strong>Buy whenever you need it.</strong> Discounts are small in absolute terms and stock is always available. No need to hold out for a sale.</dd>

    <dt>Monitor arms</dt>
    <dd><strong>Wait if you can.</strong> Monitor arms see modest but reliable Black Friday discounts. If your current setup is uncomfortable, don't suffer through weeks of waiting — but if you can wait, you'll likely save a bit.</dd>

    <dt>USB hubs</dt>
    <dd><strong>Wait.</strong> Hubs are heavily discounted during Black Friday due to fierce competition among manufacturers.</dd>
  </dl>

  <h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>
  <div class="faq-list">
    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>When do Black Friday desk deals start on Amazon?</summary>
      <p>Amazon typically begins rolling out early Black Friday deals the week before Thanksgiving. In 2026, that means deals may start appearing around November 20, with the heaviest discounts landing on Black Friday itself (November 27) and continuing through Cyber Monday (November 30). Deals often go live in waves, so checking back multiple times during the week is worthwhile.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>Do I need Amazon Prime for Black Friday deals?</summary>
      <p>No. The vast majority of Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals on Amazon are available to all customers, not just Prime members. Amazon occasionally reserves early access to certain Lightning Deals for Prime subscribers, but the core Black Friday pricing applies to everyone. Without Prime, you'll pay standard shipping rates unless your order meets the free-shipping threshold.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>Is Cyber Monday better than Black Friday for desk gear?</summary>
      <p>They're usually comparable for desk accessories. Cyber Monday (November 30, 2026) often carries equivalent or even slightly better deals on tech peripherals like mice, keyboards, and USB hubs. If you miss Black Friday, Cyber Monday is worth checking before you assume you've missed out entirely.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>How do I set up a price drop alert on CamelCamelCamel?</summary>
      <p>Go to <a href="https://camelcamelcamel.com" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">camelcamelcamel.com</a> and create a free account. Then paste the Amazon product URL into the search bar. On the product's price history page, you'll see a "Set alert" form where you can enter your desired price. CamelCamelCamel will send you an email when the price drops to or below that target.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>What if I buy something and the price drops further?</summary>
      <p>Amazon's standard return window lets you return most items within 30 days of delivery. During the holiday season, Amazon typically extends this window through late January. If you spot a lower price shortly after buying, you can return the original order and repurchase at the new price. Some credit cards also offer price protection — check with your card issuer for details.</p>
    </details>
  </div>

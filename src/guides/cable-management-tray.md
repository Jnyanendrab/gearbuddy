  <h2 id="how-to-choose">How to choose a cable tray</h2>
  <p>Five criteria to consider before ordering:</p>

  <ol>
    <li>
      <strong>Clamp-on vs. screw-mount.</strong> Clamp-on trays grip the desk edge with adjustable brackets — no tools, no drilling, no permanent marks. They're reversible and renter-friendly. Screw-mount trays bolt directly to the underside of the desk, offering more stability and higher weight capacity, but they leave small pilot holes. If you own your desk and want a permanent install, screw-mount is solid. If you rent or rearrange often, clamp-on is the safer bet.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Desk thickness.</strong> Measure your desk edge before ordering. Most clamp-on trays fit desks up to approximately 5 cm thick. Thick hardwood or bamboo tops may exceed that range and require a wider clamp or a screw-mount alternative instead.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Tray material.</strong> Metal mesh allows airflow around warm power bricks and surge protectors, which helps keep electronics cooler. Plastic trays are lighter and often cheaper, but they can flex or sag under a loaded power strip over time. For anything holding a full strip and multiple adapters, metal mesh is the more durable choice.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Length.</strong> Match the tray length to your power strip. Common trays run 40–60 cm long, and most standard power strips fall within that range. Measure your strip before ordering — a tray that's too short forces the strip to hang awkwardly or not fit at all.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Weight capacity.</strong> A loaded power strip with multiple adapters and charging bricks can weigh 1–2 kg. Most metal clamp-on trays are rated for 3–5 kg, which is comfortable for a typical home office setup. If you plan to store a heavy UPS or multiple strips, check the specific product's rating.
    </li>
  </ol>
</section>

  <h2 id="workspace-safety">Workspace organization and safety</h2>
  <p>Cable clutter isn't just an aesthetic problem — it's a genuine safety concern. Loose cables on the floor create tripping hazards, especially in shared workspaces or rooms with foot traffic. Cables that sit on the floor also collect dust and debris, which can accumulate around warm power bricks and reduce airflow. Over time, dust buildup around power adapters and surge protectors contributes to overheating risk.</p>

  <p>When routing cables, keep power cables (AC) separated from data cables (USB, HDMI, Ethernet) where practical. Running them in parallel within the same tight bundle can cause electromagnetic interference (EMI) that degrades signal quality on unshielded data cables. Crossing them at right angles when paths intersect, rather than running them side by side, minimizes this effect.</p>

  <p>For more detailed guidance on workspace safety and ergonomics, these resources are worth bookmarking:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OSHA Computer Workstations eTool</a></li>

  <h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

  <div class="faq-list">
    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>Will a cable tray damage my desk?</summary>
      <p>Clamp-on trays use rubber-padded or felt-lined clamp jaws that grip the desk edge without scratching or permanently marking the surface. No drilling or adhesive is involved, and removing the tray leaves zero trace. Screw-mount trays do require small pilot holes (typically 3–4 mm in diameter) in the underside of the desk, but these are invisible during normal use. If any permanent modification concerns you, stick with a clamp-on design.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>How much weight can a clamp-on tray hold?</summary>
      <p>Based on manufacturer specifications and publicly available user reviews, most metal mesh clamp-on trays are rated for approximately 3–5 kg. A typical power strip with several adapters and charging bricks attached weighs around 0.5–1.5 kg, which falls well within that range. If you plan to store a heavy UPS battery backup or multiple power strips, check the specific product's rated capacity before purchasing. Screw-mount trays generally support more weight than clamp-on alternatives.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>Can I use a cable tray with a standing desk?</summary>
      <p>Yes. Both clamp-on and screw-mount trays attach to the underside of the desk and move with it as the desk rises and lowers. The key consideration is the power cable running from the floor outlet to your power strip: make sure it's long enough to reach at the desk's maximum height, with a loose loop of slack so it doesn't pull taut or strain the connection. Some standing desk users add a cable chain or drag chain accessory to manage this vertical cable run as the desk moves.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>Do I need a cable tray if I have a monitor arm?</summary>
      <p>A monitor arm and a cable tray solve two different, complementary problems. The arm's integrated cable channels manage the cables between your monitors and your desk — HDMI, DisplayPort, and USB cables routed inside the arm structure. A cable tray manages everything at desk level and below: your power strip, laptop charger, USB hub, and charging bricks. If your only cable clutter is from monitor cables, the arm alone may be enough. But if you also have a tangle of power and peripheral cables, you'll want both.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="faq-item">
      <summary>What's the easiest way to label my cables?</summary>
      <p>Adhesive label tape or a label maker works well for permanent setups — print a short label (e.g., "Monitor HDMI" or "Laptop charger") and wrap it around each cable near the plug end. For setups that change frequently, color-coded velcro cable ties are faster: assign one color per device category (e.g., blue for monitors, green for peripherals) and you can identify any cable at a glance without reading a label. Labeling both ends of each cable saves time when tracing a cable back to its source.</p>
    </details>
  </div>

How to choose a monitor light bar: 5 criteria

  1. Auto-dimming vs. manual. An auto-dimming bar has a built-in ambient light sensor that reads the brightness in your room and adjusts the bar's output automatically. A manual bar requires you to tap a brightness control every time the room lighting changes — morning sun, afternoon clouds, evening darkness. If you work near a window or in a room where lighting shifts throughout the day, auto-dimming saves real effort.
  2. Color temperature range. Look for a range of 2700K–6500K. The warm end (2700K) produces an amber tone suited to evening use, while the cool end (6500K) gives a crisp, daylight-like white useful for detail work or color-critical tasks. A wider range gives you more flexibility to match the bar to natural light conditions.
  3. Power source. Most monitor light bars run on USB-A, drawing power from your monitor's built-in USB port or any standard USB charger. Before buying, check that your monitor has a USB-A port — or plan to use a separate USB charger or a port on your desktop PC. No separate power outlet is typically needed.
  4. Rear ambient light. A dark wall behind a bright monitor forces your eyes to constantly adjust between extremes of brightness. Rear ambient lighting (bias lighting) raises the luminance around the screen edges, softening that contrast. Among the two bars in this guide, only the BenQ ScreenBar Halo has built-in rear ambient light.
  5. Monitor compatibility. Check the bar's length against your monitor's width. A bar that's much shorter than the monitor leaves the outer desk areas dim. Also check the clamp's thickness range against your monitor's top bezel — most bars fit bezels between roughly 1 cm and 3 cm thick, but very thin or very thick bezels may not work.

Eye strain and desk lighting

Eye strain at a computer workstation often comes from two sources: uneven lighting and screen glare. When overhead lighting or a desk lamp creates bright spots on your monitor, your eyes constantly adjust between the bright reflections and the dimmer screen content. At the same time, if your keyboard and desk sit in shadow while your monitor blazes, the contrast forces your pupils to adapt every time you look from screen to desk and back.

A monitor light bar addresses both problems by providing even illumination across your desk surface without adding any light to the screen panel. Models with rear ambient lighting go further by reducing the contrast between a bright display and a dark wall behind it. Consistent, even lighting around your workspace lets your eyes relax rather than constantly readjusting.

Frequently asked questions

Do monitor light bars reduce eye strain?

They can help. Monitor light bars address two common contributors to eye strain: uneven task lighting and high contrast between a bright screen and dark surroundings. The asymmetric beam illuminates your desk without adding glare to the monitor, and models with a rear ambient halo soften the bright-screen-against-dark-wall contrast. Many users report more comfortable extended sessions after adding a light bar, though individual results depend on room setup, screen brightness, and personal sensitivity.

Can I use a monitor light bar with a curved monitor?

Yes. Most standard bars clip onto curved monitors without issues, though a straight bar on a deep curve may not sit perfectly flush. The BenQ ScreenBar Halo is specifically designed for curved and ultrawide displays — its clamp and form factor are engineered to sit stably on curved panels. If you have a tightly curved ultrawide (1000R or similar), check the manufacturer's compatibility notes before purchasing.

How is a monitor light bar different from a desk lamp?

The key difference is beam direction. A desk lamp radiates light in many directions, including toward your screen, where it creates glare and reflections. A monitor light bar uses asymmetric optics that bend the LED output so it only travels downward and forward onto the desk. No light hits the screen. A light bar also mounts on top of the monitor, freeing up the desk space a lamp base would otherwise occupy.

What color temperature should I use for work?

For daytime productivity — writing, coding, general office tasks — a neutral 4000K–5000K is a common starting point because it approximates natural daylight. For evening sessions, switching to a warmer 2700K–3000K reduces blue-light exposure and feels more relaxed. If you do color-critical creative work (photo editing, video grading), a cooler 5000K–6500K more closely matches standard daylight viewing conditions. Adjust to personal comfort; there's no single correct answer.

Does the BenQ ScreenBar Halo need a power outlet?

No. The ScreenBar Halo is powered via a standard USB-A cable. You can plug it into a USB port on your monitor, desktop PC, laptop, or any USB wall charger. No dedicated power outlet is required, which simplifies cable management. Standard 5V USB-A ports are generally sufficient for the bar's LED array.
